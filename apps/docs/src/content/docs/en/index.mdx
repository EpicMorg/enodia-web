**enodia** asks your deployed services what version they're running,
checks those versions against vendor lifecycle calendars, and tells you
what's already dead, what's dying, and where your fleet has drifted
apart. You describe your services once — enodia handles the rest.

:::caution[Status: pre-1.0]
enodia is implemented and used against real production infrastructure —
collection, evaluation, 29 probes, reporting — but no tagged release
exists yet, and the config schema may still change before one does. See
[Getting started](/en/getting-started/) for what actually works today.
:::

## Why enodia

Most tools cover one half of the problem: they either track upstream
releases, or they know what you've deployed — rarely both, and none of
them join it against vendor lifecycle dates.

| Tool | Tracks upstream releases | Knows what you deployed | Lifecycle dates |
|---|---|---|---|
| `eol` CLI | — | — | yes |
| nvchecker | yes | — | — |
| Renovate / Dependabot | yes (dependencies) | — | — |
| what's-up-docker | yes (containers only) | containers only | — |
| Uptime Kuma | — | up/down only | — |
| **enodia** | yes | **yes** | **yes** |

## A minute of enodia

```yaml title="enodia.yaml"
schemaVersion: 1
targets:
  - id: jira-main
    product: jira
    address: https://jira.example.com

  - id: gitlab-main
    product: gitlab
    address: https://gitlab.example.com
    credentials: gitlab-token

credentials:
  gitlab-token:
    kind: token-header
    header: PRIVATE-TOKEN
    value: "${GITLAB_TOKEN}"
```

```console
$ enodia check
ID           PRODUCT  PATCH   LIFECYCLE  BRANCH     SEVERITY  REASON
jira-main    jira     behind  active     newer_lts  warn      -
gitlab-main  gitlab   behind  eol        newer      fail      -
```

That's the whole loop: describe a target once, run `enodia check`, get
back three independent facts per target — patch status, lifecycle
status, and whether a newer branch exists — plus a severity computed
from policy you control.

## The name

Enodia, "she of the wayside", is an epithet of Hecate: torchbearer,
keeper of crossroads. Fitting for something that lights up what is
decaying and stands where you choose which way to upgrade.
